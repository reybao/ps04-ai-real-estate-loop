# Editorial Policy: The AI Real Estate Loop

## Purpose and audience

Select evidence that helps real estate, architecture, and engineering students and early-career professionals understand two connected questions: how AI is changing U.S. commercial real estate, and how land, power, construction capacity, financing, and regulation shape AI infrastructure.

## Mechanical eligibility

- Use only material inside the run's inclusive 30-calendar-day UTC window.
- Require a verifiable publication date and substantive accessible content.
- Treat an unknown, invalid, or future date as mechanically ineligible.
- Reused evidence may remain eligible when it is still inside the window; `unchanged` is not a rejection reason.
- Reuploads, syndicated copies, and cross-platform copies of the same recording count as one underlying item.

## Editorial criteria

Evaluate each eligible item on five dimensions, each scored from 0 to 2:

1. Audience relevance: does it change a decision, risk assessment, or professional workflow for the target audience?
2. Evidence directness: does it contain primary documents, data, a specific case, or a locatable statement?
3. Claim-specific credibility: can this source support this particular claim, independent of institutional reputation or popularity?
4. Novelty: does it add a new fact, update, counterexample, or interpretation beyond the existing evidence store?
5. Perspective contribution: does it add a regulatory, research, operating, commercial, financing, engineering, labor, or community perspective?

Normally accept items scoring 8-10 that can support an explicit claim. Defer items with unresolved dates, identity, methods, access, or corroboration. Reject items that are off-topic, duplicative, promotional without usable evidence, or unsupported.

## Source credibility and commercial incentives

- Government records, regulatory filings, grid-operator materials, planning records, audited disclosures, and original research may support verifiable facts within their scope.
- Independent reporting may support facts and context when it identifies sources and methods.
- Practitioner interviews, podcasts, and conference videos may support attributed experience and interpretation, not universal conclusions.
- Company announcements and vendor demonstrations establish what the company claims or launched. They do not independently prove effectiveness, feasibility, schedule, or return on investment.
- Popularity, subscriber counts, view counts, and model confidence are not evidence of truth.
- Multiple stories repeating one announcement form one claim cluster, not independent corroboration.

## Uncertainty and conflicting evidence

- Distinguish `reported_fact`, `source_claim`, and `inference`.
- Preserve material uncertainty in decisions, briefs, articles, and scripts.
- When sources conflict, identify differences in date, market, method, definition, and commercial interest before drawing a conclusion.
- Do not invent missing evidence or convert announced capacity into delivered capacity.
- Every public factual claim must resolve to an exact retained source version and a text quote or transcript segment.

## Exclusions

Exclude general AI news without a direct real-estate or data-center connection, routine residential-market coverage, short-term stock recommendations, inaccessible headline-only material, unsupported promotional claims, and content that merely repeats another source without an independent contribution.

## Human review

AI decisions are recommendations. The student reviews at least three accepted and three rejected or deferred items, records disagreements and factual corrections, revises this policy at least once, reruns the affected stage, and supplies the only final publication approval.
